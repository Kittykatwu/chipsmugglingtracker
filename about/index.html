About

Tracking what happens when people are caught smuggling advanced AI chips to China.

The project

AI Chip Smuggling Enforcement Tracker is an open, case-level and defendant-level record of criminal and regulatory enforcement against people and companies accused of diverting advanced AI chips, or servers containing them, to China in violation of export controls. It records who has been charged, where, with what, and what happened next, from court records and official statements.

It exists to give journalists and researchers accurate, citable numbers about the enforcement response. It does not estimate how much hardware has been smuggled. The data is free to reuse under CC BY 4.0.

Author

Katherine Wu, J.D. candidate, Stanford Law School (Stanford profile).

Katherine Wu is a J.D. candidate at Stanford Law School, where she is editor in chief of the Stanford Space & Policy Journal and an editor of the Stanford Journal of Law, Economics & Business. She received the Gerald Gunther Prize for Outstanding Performance and has served as a judicial intern at the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit. She built this tracker out of curiosity about what happens to AI chip smuggling cases after the initial headlines, and to make the underlying court records easier for journalists, researchers and the public to find and understand.

How the data is kept accurate

  • Every case and defendant entry links to its source, preferably a court record or an official government release.
  • A person's status is changed to a guilty plea, conviction or sentence only on the basis of a court record or official release, never news reports alone.
  • Every change to the data is logged publicly, with the old value, the new value and the source, on Updates.
  • The headline numbers are recalculated from the published data every time the site is rebuilt. See the methodology.
